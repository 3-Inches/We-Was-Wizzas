# Ars Magica Saga Toolkit

A browser app for running an *Ars Magica* Fifth Edition saga under the Definitive Edition rules. It builds characters and covenants, checks the rules as you go, and does the in-play arithmetic. All of its rules data is generated from the markdown books in this repository.

It runs locally in the browser. Data is stored in IndexedDB and shared as files or links, and nothing goes to a server.

## What it does

**Learning the toolkit:**

- **Tutorials** (Guide page): thirteen guided walk-throughs of the real app: a tour of the toolkit, then starting a saga, choosing its optional rules (and where each one shows), founding the covenant (its aura too), making a magus, a companion and a grog, using the guided build, designing a spell and a power, building a laboratory and improving it in play, playing a season (free and working seasons, the year's end), and Quick build for the storyguide. A small panel explains each step, points at the control with a pulsing outline, and Next goes to the right page (and the right wizard step); the player does the clicking.
  - Where the toolkit can do the work, a step offers to: **Build the whole character for me** (a complete, legal draft from the concept themes), **Use the Medium package for me**, **Give each magus a standard lab**. Each can be undone.
  - A tutorial can step into another and come back: the character tutorials' Concept step walks through the guided build, and returns to the Review step if the guided build made the draft. Reaching the guided build in any tutorial offers the same side trip.
  - A tutorial that makes something can run in a practice saga, leaving real sagas alone. The panel can be shrunk or ended at any time; on a phone it keeps to a third of the screen, with its buttons in one row.
- **Hints for new players:** short tips at the places people get stuck (the wizard steps, the Point balance, experience pools, the covenant's Build Points, the Seasons page, the Market, spell design and more), each hidden with **Got it**. They can be turned off in the sidebar's Display settings, and the Guide page brings hidden ones back. They step aside while a tutorial runs.
- **In the app:** each Game mechanics topic says where the toolkit does it, with a link when it is a page of its own; a topic an optional rule governs names the rule and whether the saga last opened uses it.
- **The Guide page** links every area of Game mechanics, answers the common questions, and lists every optional rule with what it adds, its book, where it shows and whether it starts on, drawn from the rules list itself.
- Arithmetic the app does for you (Build Point costs, covenfolk numbers, Certamen, lab Safety, Twilight rolls, prices) is summed up in a line, with the working behind a **How it works** button.

**Quick build** (Tools → Quick build): the toolkit does the heavy lifting, for players and the storyguide alike.

- **Characters:** whole grogs, companions, magi and Mythic Companions, one to eight at a time. Pick a role (shield grog, archer, sergeant, servant, craftsman, chirurgeon, scribe, huntsman, groom; knight, mercenary captain, scholar, priest, merchant, master craftsman, spy, woodsman, physician, courtier, cunning folk, demon hunter; the magus concept themes; the Mythic Companion types) or leave it to chance, and say how much else is left to chance (keep to the role, vary it, anything goes). The role sets the concept, Social Status and gear; the guided build's auto-build makes the rest of the choices; the result passes the rules check (the generator tries the next seed if one roll doesn't).
  - An NPC is finished and ready to play, aged with a roll for each year past 35 (a magus with a Longevity Ritual from their own Lab Total), and listed apart in the sidebar; a player's character is a draft at the Review step.
  - Names, homeland and society come from the saga's part of Mythic Europe (magi take Latin names). A Social Status the rules give to one sex only (Knight, Priest) decides the character's sex unless one is chosen; then the status is left out.
  - With the guild rules on, craftsmen take a guild rank, its Social Status and Guild Lore (*City & Guild*). A magus in the Rhine, Thebes or Transylvania gets that Tribunal's rank and gild, citizenship and phyle, or privilege, when its customs are on.
  - A magus can be given spells of chosen Arts (up to a level, rituals or not), spent from the apprenticeship's spell levels within the creation limit, and a familiar suited to their best Arts, bound before play when their Lab Total reaches its level, with the cords bought from that Lab Total.
  - Every roll has a seed, shown under **How it was built**, so the same choices and seed make the same character.
- **Fill out the covenant:** choose which of the saga's covenants (the one with the most magi comes first), then what to fill, covering everything on the Covenfolk page:
  - a steward, chamberlain (or one autocrat for both), turb captain and a skilled scribe; a teacher of three or four Abilities at one score, the ones the covenant's people lack most, older, with Communication +3 and Teaching to match (only the highest score counts toward the cost), with Good Teacher if wanted and teaching in the lab with the best Teaching Specialization; a skilled bookbinder and illuminator; a drill sergeant and a unit sergeant for each body of soldiers;
  - cost-saving craftsmen: carpenters first (they save in Buildings and Consumables), then each craft where it saves most, at the Craft score that saves most for the Build Points, then swaps while that saves more; with laborers re-balanced around them;
  - grogs for the turb as full characters (a sergeant first), soldiers for each soldier Boon, the servants, teamsters, laborers and horses the covenant needs, its weapons and armor points worked out from the turb, Redcap visits and a cycle of feast days;
  - books for the library, chosen for the experience they give the covenant's magi above what they know and the books already on the shelf (an Exceptional Book's Art gets nothing below its level): primers and deeper summae at the best Quality their Level allows, across Techniques and Forms, tractatus, Ability summae above what the teacher teaches, Laboratory Texts spread across Technique and Form pairs (no Art more than twice), and subjects chosen for a base package's books; the library takes up to a chosen share of the Build Points left;
  - the Build Points left at the end as vis sources to detail, vis in store, money, or a third of each.
  As starting covenfolk bought with Build Points, or recruited in play. Under the Guild Hook its craftsmen get guild ranks and the guild its pension and wages. No name is given twice in a saga. A preview shows each addition with its Build Points, the Build Points by kind before and after, what each Hidden, Flawed or Illusory Resource pays for, and anything the rules check would still flag on a grog, before anything changes; what the Build Points cannot cover is left to recruit in play.
  - **Library:** summae and tractatus on the Arts the magi use most (a level above what the library holds), summae on Magic Theory, Parma Magica and two other Abilities, Laboratory Texts of spells in their Arts, and mundane books, which cost nothing.
  - **Budgets:** the covenant's Build Points can be set aside per kind (specialists, books, vis, items, labs, money); the fill-out keeps to what is left. A Hidden, Flawed or Illusory Resource's pool can be set aside for any of those kinds, covenfolk included, and the fill-out spends from it (dearest first) once its own budget runs out. A Flawed Resource whose story has been told is no longer counted.
- **Creatures & encounters:** a bestiary of 422 creatures taken from the stat blocks of the core book and the reviewed supplements (`tools/extract/extract_bestiary.py`), filtered by Realm, Might and text, and by the saga's books. **Roll one** picks at random; each shows its full stat block and links to its page. The storyguide's **cast** keeps the ones to use, with how many and notes.
- The generators are also a test bench: the tests build every role in several Tribunals at every level of randomness and check each against the rules.

**Characters.** A step-by-step wizard covers grogs, companions, Mythic Companions and magi:

- It tracks the Virtue/Flaw balance, Characteristic points, and every experience pool: native language, childhood, later life, apprenticeship, post-Gauntlet, and pools granted by Virtues.
- It applies Affinities, Puissance, the age cap, House benefits, the Ex Miscellanea traditions, and spell levels learnable at creation.
- It enforces who may take each Virtue or Flaw: character type, House, The Gift, gender, prerequisites, incompatible combinations, Characteristic and age limits, and Virtues meant only for faeries, magic beings or hedge traditions. Virtues that bring a Flaw with them (Blood of the Nephilim and Greedy, Diedne Magic and a Major Story Flaw) add it for you, and Virtues with a sub-choice (Faerie Blood heritage, Corrupted Arts) offer a dropdown.
- An Affinity added after experience was spent keeps the scores you bought and frees the extra xp, instead of silently raising them.
- Virtues that change a budget can be added or removed at any time: Wealthy, Poor, Skilled or Weak Parens, and Virtues with their own xp pool. When a budget shrinks, the excess is taken back from the most recently added Abilities and the app tells you what moved.
- Every Virtue and Flaw is tagged from its rules: character type, House, The Gift, what it adds (free or counted) and what it needs. Tags can be searched and clicked to filter. "Only what this character can take" also hides picks that would break a creation limit, such as the 10 Flaw points or 5 Minor Flaws.
- A rules check cites the book for each issue. Click an issue to fix it:
  - It adds or removes a Virtue, sets a value, moves or takes back xp, or shows the dropdown for a choice.
  - When the troupe has to decide, it opens the step where the decision is made.
  - "Resolve all" previews every automatic fix before applying it, and every fix can be undone.
  - Any issue can instead be allowed as a troupe ruling. Covenant issues get the same fix buttons.
- Fields the rules read are dropdowns with an "Other" option to type your own: Tribunal, homeland (which also sets the society), Characteristic descriptions, and the type of Craft, Profession, Language, Lore or Organization. Gender is Male or Female, the two the Virtue and Flaw rules refer to.
- The Ability list hides what the character cannot learn: Supernatural Abilities without their Virtue, House Abilities (Heartbeast, Enigmatic Wisdom, Faerie Magic), and Mystery Cult Lore for non-initiates.
- Post-Gauntlet years give 30 xp each, less 10 per season of lab work, never below 0 in a year (DE p.50).
- Characters older than 35 roll aging before play, one year at a time or all at once, with Living Conditions, the Longevity Ritual, crises, and undo. Aging does not change what the Characteristics cost at creation.
- Powers from Greater, Lesser, Personal and Ritual Power, Focus Power and Heroes' Birthright are built against their level budgets, with Penetration, Initiative, Fatigue and Confidence cost, or copied from a spell; the spell list shows what each spell does. Strong Angelic Heritage can be taken more than once; each copy adds 30 levels of holy powers, paid from the Divine Might it gives (Corpus, a twentieth of the character's age), which also counts as Magic Resistance.
- Holy Magic replaces Magic Theory in Lab Totals and the creation checks. True Faith and relics give Magic Resistance, and only the highest source counts.
- Recommendations follow the concept themes you pick.
- A Virtue that gives another adds it free and keeps it in step: Strong Faerie Blood gives Second Sight; Magical Blood gives the kind of magic being's advantage (+1 Characteristic, a Supernatural Virtue or a power); Templar Commander gives Temporal Influence. If the character already paid for that Virtue, it becomes free.
- Every Virtue and Flaw description ends with **Rules it refers to**: the Virtues and Flaws it names and the book sections it cites ("see page 225"), quoted in place.
- Each step has a short note on how its Characteristics, Virtues, Abilities, Arts or spells can be gained or improved in play (Characteristic Rituals, teaching Hermetic Virtues, Initiation, Twilight…).
- Companion Flaws (Magical Animal, Realm Spirit, Animal Companion) create a linked sheet for the companion, with Might, Size and powers.
- A magical companion follows *Realms of Power: Magic*'s rules for magic characters:
  - Experience comes from the Season (spring 120, summer 360, autumn 720, winter 1200) instead of age, and the character does not age.
  - Might comes from the saga's power level and the character's role, less Size for an animal. It buys Magic Qualities, Inferiorities give more points, and grogs take no Major Qualities.
  - Size changes Strength and Quickness, Might is subtracted from Advancement Totals, and a magic human has Monstrous Appearance unless it takes Natural Appearance.
  - Mundane Social Statuses give way to the five magical ones (a companion granted by a Flaw is a Magical Friend), and a companion cannot have companions of its own.
  - The Creature card (on the sheet, and on the Virtues step while creating) lists the creation steps in the book's order, with how far each has got: Characteristics, Virtues and Flaws, Season and Abilities, Might and Qualities, powers, finishing touches.
  - Repeatable Qualities are added several at a time and shown once with a count. Each Quality's limits are checked, with a fix that removes the copies that do not count:
    - Improved (Characteristic) needs the Characteristic at +5 or more first.
    - Reduced (Characteristic) needs it at −5 or less from Poor (Characteristic), and no lower than minus the greatest Characteristic.
    - Reduced Fatigue and Reduced Abilities have their caps; Limited Gestures is not for animals.
    - Inferiorities that do not count give no points.
  - Removing the Flaw an Inferiority brought removes the Inferiority and its points.
  - Improved Confidence raises the Confidence Score and points, also on a finished character.
  - Magical Meditation and (Form) Resistance give their Supernatural Ability at 1.
  - Improved Powers gives 5 mastery points each, and unused power levels give 1 per 5 levels for powers of their kind. They are spent on each power's Might cost (5 more Penetration a point), its Initiative, or a Focus Power's level beyond Might, or turned into 5 experience points each in Penetration, Concentration or Finesse right on the powers card. Reduced Power takes 5 away; only it lets mastery raise a cost or lower Initiative.
  - Magical Master, Champion and Covenfolk combine with another Social Status, so a companion Flaw's being can have one alongside the Magical Friend the Flaw asks for.
  - A magic character has no free native language: its Season experience buys any language it knows.
  - A magic character's power Penetration is Might − 5 × its Might cost + Penetration, and effects of level 50 or more must be Ritual Powers.
- **Guided build** (Concept step): the player rates statements about what they want to play, 0–10 (skipping counts as 5), and gets Virtues, Flaws, a House, Arts and Abilities recommended from every enabled book, then a complete draft that passes the rules check.
  - Every Virtue and Flaw has a record of outcome tags (Casting Totals, Penetration, Faerie, the Church…) with a direction and strength, built from the hand-written mechanics and the book text, plus region and culture gates.
  - Follow-up questions open at 7 or more (or 3 or less) and stop once fewer than 10 options remain. Concept themes pre-set answers; a covenant in the toolkit answers the questions about vis, money, the library and the aura.
  - For magi, what they do comes first: a starter question on faster progression versus flat bonuses (and why the one Major Hermetic Virtue matters most), then each activity (casting, the lab), the outputs within it (combat casting, Penetration, spontaneous magic, rituals; inventing, lab texts, enchanting, longevity, familiars, experimenting, vis extraction, teaching), then broad versus narrow (a Focus, one Art, or a weakness accepted elsewhere), steady versus cyclic magic, and a pick-any archetype question (Elementalist, Shapeshifter, Healer…).
  - Each question ends with what its best options give, Minor then Major Virtues, then Minor then Major Flaws ("Minor Virtues: Life Boost (+5 per Fatigue level spent, even into Wounds)…").
  - Options that move a Total (Casting, Lab, Penetration, spontaneous magic, progression) are ranked against the others that move the same Total by the size of their edge.
  - Each option shows its fit (how well it matches the answers) and its strength (what it is worth in this saga, in seasons over the first years). Mechanical Flaws are suggested for what the player rated low, Story and Personality Flaws for what they rated high, and a Flaw that hurts something rated high never.
  - A tie-breaker round when too much is rated 9–10, House recommendations, explainers and "Still interested?" for complex options, and Browse by tag.
  - The draft records why each choice was made ("from: I expect to cast in a fight — 9"). The storyguide sets the saga's realms, speed and politics once on the saga settings page.

**Covenants:**

- Hooks & Boons, including the DE situation packages.
- Build Points for the library (with the creation limits), lab texts, casting tablets, vis sources and stocks, enchanted items, specialists and teachers, and laboratories.
- Laboratory personalization: Size, Refinement, lab Virtues & Flaws, Specializations and Upkeep.
  - Entries whose bonus depends on something take a number (a Guardian's or Horde's Intelligence, a Servant's, an Assistant's Intelligence + Magic Theory, a Familiar's Golden Cord, a Studio's display). Either/or entries take a version (Magical Heating and Lighting as the Superior or Excessive version without Upkeep). Conditions can be switched off (Relocation's Warping). Specialization points offer only the allowed choices.
  - Every Characteristic shows how it is worked out; Safety includes the occupied-Size penalty (DE: base Safety = Refinement − occupied Size).
- Covenfolk inhabitant points, yearly finances and Loyalty. Members, labs and the aura feed into each character's totals.
  - The aura as it stands, on the Overview's Aura card: its Aura Boons, a Weak Aura (−1), a Missing Aura (none), a Faerie Aura (Faerie), a Flickering Aura's missed year (−1) and changes that last a season. Changes are recorded with their reason and kept in its history, with the core book's guide to how auras grow and fade (a covenant's magic over decades, a creature of Might 30 or more, a congregation's Dominion, sin, stories).
  - **Aligned auras** (*Realms of Power: Magic*): a Magic aura aligned with an Art counts double for Casting Scores and Lab Totals that use it (and a creature of that Form spends a Might Point less on a Power there); a Healing aura adds half its strength to Recovery Totals; Luck, Distracting and an emotion are noted with their effect at the aura's strength. The Fine Aura and Favorable Aura Boons and the Troublesome Aura Hook are among the Site Boons and Hooks. An Infernal aura takes its strength off wound and disease recovery, and no disease tended there improves (*Realms of Power: The Infernal*).
  - **A Dominion and its congregation** (*Realms of Power: The Divine*): 3 from the rite, 4 after a year of regular worship, 5 after a decade for a large congregation in a significant church, a point more for each Faith Point a relic has over five; when worship stops it fades steadily over as long as it was kept. The year's end moves the aura with it.
  - **Aura variation** (*Realms of Power: Magic*, optional): a Magic aura's base score from its tethers, and each year's modifier (back toward the base, stronger auras pressing on it, the covenant's magic by its season, vis spent three pawns at a time in study, rituals and renewed wards, seasons of vis extraction, magical botches); at ±4 the year's end rolls on the Aura Strengthening or Weakening Table, with botch dice that build up until a regio fractures (3 Warping Points for everyone living there). A change recorded as a new tether moves the base too.
  - Income sources follow the Boons and Hooks (Wealth, Secondary Income, Poverty, Natural Disaster, Indebted, Right, Contested Resource), with the book's source types and yearly Income Modification. Tithes and Tithing Miracles are set per income and vis source, and what the covenant receives is rounded up.
  - Hidden, Flawed and Illusory Resources each pay for 250 Build Points of chosen resources; a Flawed Resource's story can save half, lose all, or double the loss. The Exceptional Book Boon adds its summa free. Only Hooks whose text allows it can be Unknown.
  - Craftsmen choose a craft, which sets their cost saving and its limits; buttons find the number of laborers, servants, teamsters and each craft that keeps spending lowest. *Covenants* lists example crafts only: the others the books name are offered too (tailors, tanners, dyers, saddlers, farriers and cartwrights; roofers and glaziers; potters and pewterers; bakers, butchers and millers; shield makers), marked as such, so laborers (up to half of Provisions) and food crafts work side by side; a box keeps to the book's examples. Quick add makes specialists, teachers and craftsmen within the starting age limits.
  - The year's end (*Covenants*, closed on the Seasons page): a preview of the treasury after the year's balance, inflation (which stays put in a year spending fell), the year's vis gathered into stocks (skip any source), a year of familiarity for Loyalty, income changes made lasting, and the allowance to members. The Income Modification roll, the Pound of Enumerus, one-off spending presets and lab outfitting.
  - Teachers, specialists and craftsmen have Ability dropdowns and can know several Abilities (only the highest sets the cost), the Characteristics their job uses (Communication to teach, Dexterity for most crafts, Presence to lead), Good Teacher, the lab they teach in (its Teaching Specialization adds up to +3), and the Source Quality they teach at. A craftsman shows the quality of goods their Dexterity + Craft makes.
  - Roles from *Covenants* and *Grogs*: an autocrat (steward and chamberlain at once: the Loyalty of both), unit sergeants, a drill sergeant who drills the whole turb one season a year, personal servants (no Build Points, but living like specialists) and agents (bought, but not inhabitants). A custos grog counts as a specialist.
  - Each year specialists and craftsmen gain experience: Exposure in their job in the seasons they work (Teaching for a teacher's seasons of teaching), Practice in their free seasons, bringing a lesser Ability up to their best. The Wealthy Virtue and Poor Flaw set how many seasons are free (*City & Guild*: free time, not pay). Their Build Points stay what they were bought for. Their upkeep is the same per point whatever their skill.
  - **Soldiers** from the Residents Boons and Free Choices (Veteran Fighters a penny a day, Crossbowmen nine, Hunters or Sailors one, Criminals, Peasants and Cavalry unpaid), with their horses and kit; those who live at the covenant count as inhabitants. **Weapons and armor points** worked out from members' sheets, an outfit per grog at the covenant's equipment, the soldiers' kit and the stores.
  - **Each year:** Redcap visits, feast days (a cycle of four or more earns a Loyalty point every year), festivals, guests, hired hands and books, from the price list; unlike Sundry they stay from year to year.
  - **How the covenfolk live:** their organization (DE: a noble household, military, nations, guilds, professional guilds, a monastery or none), magi living impoverished or as covenfolk for fewer points, and members who pay their own upkeep (not counted).
  - **The Guild Hook:** what the covenfolk's guild wants (standard wages, a pension, decent conditions), a strike (laborers and craftsmen save nothing while it lasts), and guild ranks for every craftsman (*City & Guild*: apprentice to dean by age and Craft, with the Guild Lore, Bargain, Leadership and Artes Liberales each needs), shown too under professional guilds. Craftsmen earn Labor Points ((Dexterity + Craft) × 2, 3 or 6 a season worked, less 36 a year) to raise their workshop's Innovation and Raw Materials.
  - **Craft magic** (*Covenants*): design a craft spell from the new guidelines (Creo Animal and Herbam to preserve; Rego Animal, Herbam and Terram to work materials) or take one a magus knows, then see the Perception + Finesse roll against the craft's Ease Factor + 3 (more for a month's, season's or year's work in one casting), the chance of success and of a botch, the rolls a year and what goes wrong, the Casting Score, and the saving (a pound a magnitude a year, five a magnitude for each Ritual).
  - **The turb's day** (*Grogs*): the eight watches, each grog's patrol and watch (or shared out at random) and who is on duty, off duty or asleep at any hour, single or double duty with the share of soldiers ready; rolls to stay awake on sentry and the double-duty Fatigue roll at dusk (a lost Long-Term Fatigue level marked on the sheet); a leader's roll to train a group faster (*Lords of Men*); rewards and punishments from the *liber gregis* applied to a grog (wounds, Long-Term Fatigue, a year's better or worse rations on the aging roll, a note in their record), with the Loyalty Points one ordered by a magus costs; retirement and the pension.
  - The covenant rolls (*Grogs*): a summa on Profession: Chamberlain that grows a Level every two decades, and a tractatus of Area Lore, at no Build Points.
  - **The scriptorium** (*Covenants*): copies queued for the covenant's scribes, made in their working seasons at the year's end, corrupt when the scribe lacks the skills. Each book shows how it was made (artisans, materials, format), whether it is a copy, glossed, in a florilegium or being clarified. Books written, copied or bought in play cost no Build Points.
  - Loyalty events from the book's table (a wound by magic counts a level worse), what the Prevailing Loyalty means for the covenfolk, and unGifted council members in the Base Loyalty.
  - Vis sources: the yield guide by size, and dedicated vis (a Technique + Form pair that works as two pawns for that combination, 7.5 Build Points a pawn), as sources or stocks. Sources found in play and vis found, won or given cost no Build Points: only the stocks the covenant was founded with count. Book prices for buying and selling.

**Time, money and magic in play:**

- **Game table** (first in the saga's menu): a dashboard to run a session from. What needs attention, each with a link to where it is put right (the treasury running short by the year's end, low Loyalty, a guild strike, covenant issues, wounds, Long-Term Fatigue, illness, a workshop short of Labor Points, rules problems, anyone idle); each character's season, changed in place, with links to their Plan, Health, Cast and Lab tabs; **End the season** with its report (crises, Warping and reminders first); quick dice; each covenant's treasury, year's balance, Loyalty, vis and people, with the accounts folded; quick changes (a Loyalty event, money into or out of the treasury, vis found); **After a story**, Adventure experience for each character (logged in the season and sharing it with their other work) and Labor Points for craftsmen and merchants; **Travel, trade and intrigue** (*City & Guild*): a journey's days and cost by road, river or sea, cargo, Area Lore, ships chasing, boarding and ramming, bribes, hired criminals and sabotage, intrigue against the powerful, a house's Reputation, largesse and warehouses, and dowries, with costs paid from a purse or the treasury; and **When something comes up in play**, a list of situations each linked to the place that handles it.
- **Seasons page:** one clock for the whole saga. End the season, go to next Spring, or pass several years at once. Each character does what they logged on their sheet for the season, or else their standing activity in their free seasons, and work (Exposure) or Worship once those are spent: Exposure, practice, vis study from their purse, lab work or a lab project, workshop work, being taught by a covenant teacher, writing or copying, or an automatic choice (a magus left to the clock, or whose project set for every season is done, works the first lab project the lab and the vis allow). A season holds one activity besides an adventure, so a lab project cannot be worked twice in one season. The year's end closes every covenant's books, pays allowances, reminds you of Hooks that call for a story (Indiscreet Resource every five years), applies Dwindling Resources, rolls aging (or not: magic characters do not age; the covenant's best hand at Medicine, a specialist or a member, tends anyone in a crisis, adding their Medicine to the survival roll on Int + Medicine against 6; a failed crisis or Decrepitude 5 marks the character dead, to be unticked on Combat & Health if magic saves them), and adds the year's Warping from constant effects, a Longevity Ritual and the aura (by the time each character spends in it: always, half the time, frequent visits or rarely, set on the Warping card). A non-magus whose Warping Score rises is told what it gains (a Minor Flaw at 1 and 3, a Minor supernatural Virtue at 5, which ends the aura's Warping, and a Major Flaw from 6). A lesson the teacher cannot give (two seasons taught already, or nothing more to teach) falls back to the season the character would have had. The next season renews wards such as the Aegis of the Hearth from the covenant's vis, ends spells that have run out, and restores a magic character's Might pool. Everything can be undone, and the chronicle keeps the reports. The year's end also runs the scriptorium and finishes books whose clarification is done.
- **Magic on a character:** spells and powers that raise Characteristics or grant a Virtue are recorded on the sheet with their duration, end when it runs out, and can be ended or undone. A Characteristic raised for good by a ritual is marked as magic and costs no points, and does not unmake a magic character's Improved or Reduced (Characteristic) taken before; a lasting effect of level 30 or more gives a Warping Point unless the character cast it, it was designed for them, or they have a Might Score. A magic character's Might comes off its Advancement Totals, less 2 for each pawn of vis it eats in the season (*Plan a season*).
- **Magic on the covenant:** a magus's ritual or a magic character's Ritual Power can raise every member, teacher, specialist and craftsman at once (Creo Corpus and Creo Mentem: all physical, all mental or one Characteristic, to a maximum of 0 to +5 by level), or grant a Virtue. A magus pays Ritual vis from the covenant or their purse and rolls the casting. A Ritual Power costs Might, and then Might Score, Confidence Points, or vis with the Vis Mastery Quality (no more pawns than the Confidence Score); eating vis of the Might's Form first refills the pool. The covenant's *Magic on the covenant* card (on its Overview and on the Seasons page) lists **Recent casts**: undoing one takes the effect off everyone it reached and gives back what was spent (vis to the covenant or the purse, Might, Might Score, Confidence and Fatigue). Casts made before this was recorded can still be undone, without the refund. Before casting, the card points out targets with Might (Magic Resistance to penetrate), animals that Corpus or Mentem does not reach, and those already at the limit, who would gain only the Warping Point (with a link to leave them out).
- **Undo and redo:** every change to a saga, character or covenant can be undone, from the sidebar (or the bar at the top on a phone) or with Ctrl+Z, and redone with Ctrl+Shift+Z or Ctrl+Y. Quick edits to the same thing count as one step; the last 60 steps are kept.
- **Lab projects:** invent a spell (one designed in the saga, listed first, or one from the books; from a Laboratory Text in a season when the Lab Total reaches its level); prepare an item or talisman (vis paid a season at a time, capacity from the highest Technique + Form); attune a talisman; instill an effect (the talisman's +5, vis paid in the first season, capacity counted in pawns; a charged item in one season and for no vis, with a charge per 5 points above the level; a lesser enchantment's vis held to what its material and size could take); a Longevity Ritual for oneself or another (Lab Total 30 for someone else, halved for a subject with Difficult Longevity Ritual, a mundane gains +1 per 10 points, vis by age within twice Magic Theory); an aging crisis ends the ritual, and a magus performs it again from its Laboratory Text on the Lab & items tab, for the vis of their age then, no more of each kind than their score in that Art; bind a familiar (cords bought from the Lab Total, vis by Lab Total), strengthen the bond (only the vis not already spent, never weaker cords) or invest a power in it; extract vis; improve the lab (*Covenants*): raise its Refinement (Magic Theory at least the new Refinement + 3; an Intelligence + Magic Theory roll may gain Highly Organized, find a Hidden Defect or, on a botch, leave one; a Tidy roll may gain Spotless; a Free or Minor Flaw put right in the same season), install a lab Virtue (a season for a Minor, two for a Major, with free space and Magic Theory at least the Refinement + 3, another Virtue taken out in the same work) or put a Flaw right (a season, two for a Major, with room for what it adds), the rise in Upkeep paid from the treasury or the purse; or other lab work. Spells of the same Technique and Form can be invented alongside each other (DE, Multiple Laboratory Activities): their levels added against the lowest of their Lab Totals, from Laboratory Texts in one season. Each shows its Lab Total with the lab's General Quality, Specializations, a Magical Focus or a conditional bonus that applies, and its progress and vis; it can be worked a season at a time, set as what the character works on each season, or chosen on *Plan a season*. A lab whose Flaws rule an activity out says so in red and the work is blocked; Missing Ingredients halve the Lab Totals that need them.
  - **Experimentation:** a checkbox on every project the rules allow it (not when working from a Laboratory Text): a simple die and the risk added to the Lab Total, and a roll on the Extraordinary Results Chart, with botch dice of 1 + risk + aura, less Safety and the golden cord. No Benefit (the project abandoned if the Lab Total without the die falls short), Complete Failure (the item or familiar lost on a 0) and Disasters (a second zero destroys a long project's work, and counts as a Complete Failure on a one-season one) are applied to the project, and every roll is kept in its history.
  - **The lab each season:** an unsafe lab (negative Safety) rolls for an accident; a lab with Warping can warp what is made in it (a side effect or a modified effect); the lab's Warping Points go to the magus. Health adds to wound and recovery rolls, the Texts Specialization to the pages written a season, and a teacher's lab Teaching Specialization to the lesson.
  - A talisman gains one Shape and Material attunement a season while it is worked. When casting, the talisman attunement that fits the spell is picked from a list, and an item's constant bonuses to its bearer apply while it is worn.
- **Health and illness:** the Combat & Health tab tracks diseases (*Art & Academe*): 26 diseases from the books with their stage, Severity and Ease Factors, the avoidance roll on exposure, the Disease Penalty on every total, a physician's Prognosis Total (their Medicine counts only above the Severity), the Disease Recovery roll at each interval, and crises.
- **Agents:** a character's agency (*Houses of Hermes: Societates*): how many agents they can control, recruiting with agency experience against a would-be agent's Resistance, the Bond raised by favors or gifts (never past +3 by money), and the persuasion roll that sets an agent to a task, with its timeframe and risk.
- **More lab work:** taming a creature as a lab project (Rego of its Form with Animal Handling and Animal Ken, over consecutive seasons); non-standard lab routines (*Covenants*: Early Riser, Nocturnal, Addled and the three kinds of overtime) with their Lab Total bonus, Warping and the year's Living Conditions and extra Aging rolls; and original research toward a Breakthrough (*Houses of Hermes: True Lineages*), stabilizing Discoveries with their Warping. The library has a research roll, and the dice roller has the optional equipment bonuses of *Grogs*.
- **Free seasons** (DE, Assigning Seasons): a working character has two free seasons and two of work, a magus four; Wealthy three, Poor one, a License of Absence one more (never past four); a Regular spends one in Worship (a church's Dominion as Source Quality, for Church Lore, Concentration or Music). One who needs no sleep (the No Fatigue Quality, or set on the sheet) has two extra seasons a year for study, never lab work: the clock gives them in Spring and Autumn, and *Plan a season* takes a second activity in a season as one of them. The Seasons & Aging tab shows the year and what it has used, with the free seasons set by hand for a character the rules leave open; *Plan a season* warns when the year's free seasons are spent.
- **Optional rules:** House rules → *Optional rules* turns each supplement subsystem on or off for the saga (illness, lab routines, original research, aura variation, library research, agents, Mystery Initiation, guilds and the society tools). A rule whose book is not in the saga starts off. A card whose rule is off disappears, unless the character already has something recorded there. Each rule has a one-line summary and the book's text a click away, and a tutorial shows where each one appears.
- **Tribunal customs:** when the saga is set in a Tribunal with a 5th Edition book, the Saga page offers that Tribunal's own rules. Each stays off until the troupe ticks it, and none of them follows the saga to another Tribunal.
  - **The Rhine:** ranks (apprentice to archmage, with the voting sigils each holds and the masters needed to rise), the six gilds and their agendas, a peregrinator's hospitality, (Gild) Trained and (Gild) Enmity, and the Paths through the Forest: Forest Lore, the communion roll, and pacts whose Sacrifice (a Flaw) is taken at once and whose Fruit (a Virtue) comes when the Quest is done, with the book's four paths as presets.
  - **Thebes:** citizen or metoikos, the phylai, tokens and shards by the book's reasons, neutralizing a shard with a season's service, the warning of ostracism, eligibility for the Tribunal's Longevity Ritual, and a covenant's patron raised by the Ceremony of Propitiation (25 experience less its Might, two less penalty per pawn, paid from the stocks).
  - **Normandy:** each vis source's standing under the Perthean Compact (seisin, legacy, tropaeum, luctatio, interim lease, undeclared) with lease renewal dates, a cursor's day's march, and the Hermetic Tourney: the team's tally over the six events, prize pawns by rank, the named Sieges, and a won tropaeum added as a vis source.
  - **Transylvania:** each resident magus's privilege (cives, socius, colonus, hospes, foederatus) with what it allows and demands.
- **Mysteries & Initiation** (Seasons & Aging tab, for the Mystery Houses and anyone with a cult Lore): the House Mysteries of Bjornaer, Merinita and Verditius and the twenty Criamon stations are in the Virtue list as House-only Mystery Virtues. The card works out the Mystagogue's Initiation Total (Presence + Lore + Script, no die) against the target (15 or 21, 18 or 30 for a Virtue the Mystagogue lacks), with the books' Scripts preloaded, Ordeals that ease the next three Initiations (never below 9), a Vary roll to add a component, and Initiate, which adds the Virtue free, the Ordeal's Flaw or loss, and the season. Criamon magi follow a Path station by station (each needing House Criamon Lore one above its place); a Bjornaer records the heartbeast and rolls to change form against magic.
- **Guilds** (*City & Guild*): rank from the guild Social Status Virtue or earned in play, the Guild Lore each rank needs, the apprentice piece, a journeyman's contract roll whose Labor Points go to the workshop, the master's fee and vote, dues paid from the purse, and the dean's stipend.
- **Society** (Overview, each folded until needed): debate against another character or a storyguide opponent (*Houses of Hermes: Societates*), with the attack and defense styles, debate Fatigue, testimony, and the Argument Strength that sways allies, neutral and hostile votes; petitioning the Church (Com or Pre + Church Lore + support against the request's Ease Factor, with argument rolls); and, if turned on, a quick court verdict and etiquette performances and duels.
- **Purses and the market:** each character has a purse of pounds and vis. The covenant can give members a yearly allowance (a flat sum or a share of the surplus, and a share of the vis gathered). The Market & treasury page buys from the price lists of *Covenants* and *City & Guild* (services, hired hands, laboratories, animals, buildings, precious materials, arms, vehicles, goods), with quality and size multipliers. It also trades vis for silver at the saga's price (with the book's range and reasons, dedicated vis half again), buys Hermetic books for vis at the book prices, brings covenfolk into the covenant (showing the yearly cost), hires outside teachers (half a year's wage), and moves pounds and vis between the treasury and purses. A grog sent to haggle (*Grogs*) appraises the goods and bargains the price down (10% for every 3 points, never below half), and a bribe of a day's or a week's pay is paid from the purse.

Big topics are split into sections, such as each House Mystery or Path, or each Ability type. A rule reprinted in several books is shown once, from the book that takes precedence (the Definitive Edition, then the newest), with "Also printed in" links and the other books' versions when they differ. Each area runs from the rules every session uses to the ones that need a Virtue, House, initiation, tradition or kind of being, and character creation is grouped by the kind of character. Each entry has a summary, the key rules quoted word for word, "see also" links, and the book's own text on demand. Search covers every mechanic, and a filter shows what matters to magi, companions, grogs or the storyguide.

## Running it

Requires Node 20 or newer.

```sh
cd app
npm install
npm run dev        # http://localhost:5173
npm test           # rules engine tests (checked against the DE worked examples)
npm run build      # static site in app/dist (works from any sub-path, e.g. GitHub Pages)
```

For testing by hand or in a browser, `src/engine/testing/sagaFixture.ts` builds a whole saga from a seed: generated magi with good and harsh labs, companions who keep other hours, grogs, a library, vis, an aligned aura with Hooks, a nearby abbey with its own Dominion, and lab work of every kind in hand. The long-play test plays it for years and checks every season.

```sh
npm run qa:saga    # writes it to scripts/qa/out/saga.json (QA_SEED, QA_SEASONS, QA_TRIBUNAL, QA_OUT)
npm run qa:sweep   # imports it into the built app and visits every page and tab at 1280 and 390 pixels
```

The sweep reports page and console errors, pages wider than the screen, and "undefined" or "NaN" on a page. It needs a build and Playwright with Chromium.

`npm run dev` and `npm run build` copy the 5th Edition markdown books into `public/books` so the reader can load them.

The workflow `.github/workflows/toolkit.yml` tests and builds on pull requests. On pushes to the default branch it also deploys to GitHub Pages. For the deploy to work, enable Pages with the source set to "GitHub Actions" in the repository settings.

## How it is built

```
tools/extract/        Python extractors: markdown books -> JSON (run: npm run extract)
app/src/data/         generated JSON + hand-written mechanics for Virtues & Flaws, Houses, constants
app/src/engine/       pure rules engine (no UI): characters, magic, combat, study & aging,
                      enchantment, labs, covenants, dice, spell design, recommendations
app/src/store/        zustand store persisted to IndexedDB, migrations, export/import
app/src/ui/           React pages: character wizard & sheet, covenant, tools, reference, reader
```

- **Rules data is extracted automatically:** 1,042 Virtues & Flaws, 48 Magic Qualities and Inferiorities, 122 Abilities, 1,205 spells, 610 guidelines, 130 lab Virtues & Flaws, 36 lab features, 198 Hooks & Boons, 240 Shape & Material entries, 422 creatures' stat blocks, and weapons and armor.
- **Mechanics are written by hand** in `src/data/mechanics.ts`, one entry per Virtue or Flaw, as declarative effects: xp pools, bonuses, Lab Total modifiers and so on. Virtues and Flaws without numeric effects appear on the sheet as text for the troupe to adjudicate.
- **References are followed:** `tools/extract/extract_vf_refs.py` finds the Virtues and Flaws each text names and the pages it cites. It resolves each citation to its section through the Definitive Edition's indexes, the original core book's index mapped by topic, or the heading named in other books, plus a short list checked by hand. What those references mean for the rules (free Virtues, "the same as", requirements and exclusions) is in `src/data/crossRefs.ts`, added to the hand-written mechanics.
- **Restrictions are curated** in `src/data/restrictions.ts` from the sentences in the books that limit a Virtue or Flaw, each quoted in the rules check. `src/engine/character/restrictions.ts` applies them in both the Virtue picker and the rules check.
- **The mechanics reference was read, not guessed:** the reviewed books were read section by section into `tools/extract/mechanics_index/` (one file per part of a book). `tools/extract/build_mechanics.py` checks every entry against its book: the heading must be on its line, and each quote must appear word for word in the section. Where each mechanic is shown is decided by `mechanics_curation.py`: every area's topics in order, and rules that match an entry's book, chapter and section headings to a topic (the first that fits). `mechanics_overrides.py` corrects single entries or runs of lines (topic, section, title, or dropping what is not a rule). Abilities are taken from the Ability list and filed by type. The build writes `src/data/generated/mechanics/`: a small index plus one file per area, loaded when the area is opened. The app shows the book's own text by cutting the section out of the book file.
- **Every total carries its breakdown.** Clicking a number shows how it was calculated.
- **Books marked WIP** in this repository are still being transcribed. Content from them can contain transcription errors, and each saga can switch them off.
